English Books

Abroad in Japan

Chris Broad
I discovered Abroad in Japan nearly a year after my visit to Japan. While some videos seemed a bit too YouTubey for my taste, I found many to be genuinely entertaining and decided to give Chris Broad’s recent book a try. I didn’t expect much from a book by a YouTuber, but it turned out to be a delightful, light-hearted, and genuinely entertaining read. To be honest, I enjoyed it even more than Jonathan Clements’ A Brief History of Japan, which I read in preparation for my trip a year ago. I believe it’s worth a read even if you’ve never been to Japan or don’t plan to visit. The book is filled with humorous anecdotes, making it an effortless read that introduced me to a country and society that were completely foreign to me as a German. It manages to be both light-hearted and informative.

My rating

I’m a Stranger Here Myself

Bill Bryson
Published in 1998, this book compiles articles written by Bill Bryson for The Mail on Sunday’s Night and Day magazine. My relationship with Bryson’s works has been somewhat on-and-off, but I genuinely enjoyed this collection. The articles are concise, frequently humorous, and provide a light-hearted journey back to the decade of my birth.

My rating

The Dictator’s Handbook

Bruce Bueno de Mesquita, Alastair Smith
This book serves as the foundation for CGP Grey’s outstanding video, The Rules for Rulers. It’s a fascinating read with a unique idea that feels almost inevitable once understood, offering explanations for various phenomena. The book sheds light on many aspects and implications of the hypothesis, using examples for clarification. While I don’t always agree, it’s still a book worth reading. Despite its moderate length, I believe a longer essay would have sufficed, as the core idea is graspable even from the CGP Grey video.

My rating
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕

Immune

Philipp Dettmer
Many might be familiar with the Kurzgesagt channel, founded by Philipp Dettmer. In the channel’s well-known style, the book breaks down the fascinating and incredibly complex workings of the immune system, making it understandable for laypeople. Supported by numerous illustrations and drawings, it simplifies the subject without losing its essence. However, these necessary simplifications offer only a glimpse into the topic, and I can’t claim to have fully grasped or remembered all the elements (which is not a shortcoming of the book but inherent to the subject matter).

My rating
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The Rise and Reign of the Mammals

Steve Brusatte
The book is the second installment of The Rise and Fall series. While the first part focuses on dinosaurs (and who doesn’t find dinosaurs fascinating?), this book delves into the origins and evolution of mammals, including humans. The author is clearly knowledgeable in this field and guides readers through this prehistoric journey in an engaging manner.

Nexus

Yuval Noah Harari
I think I’m kind of over Harari. I really did enjoy Sapiens and, to some extent, Homo Deus. I really did not care for 21 Lessons for the 21st Century, and this one I liked even less. So much so that I didn’t actually finish. I think what annoys me is that it’s easier to tell a riveting story about something I know basically nothing about (like the distant past) than about times I experienced myself (the present) or times no one knows anything about (the future).

German Books

Das einzige Buch, das du über Finanzen lesen solltest

Thomas Kehl, Mona Linke
Compared to the Gerd Kommer books I read in 2023, this book is significantly less technical but more accessible and easier to digest. The core concept is straightforward: reduce debt, save, and invest. It recommends a passive investment strategy through ETFs. This book is particularly suitable for individuals who have yet to engage with their finances and retirement planning, making it a commendable choice for this audience.
